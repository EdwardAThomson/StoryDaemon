# Dev Log

## 2026-09-12

A day driven entirely by reading live runs rather than by tests, and each thing read produced a fix. It opened with an auditability gap: the first live sectioned-writing run worked (three sections, 8/8 markers, every section within 12% of its word target) but the scene it produced left no record of its own structure, since `skeleton_compliance` was computed, printed and discarded and the skeleton was never written down at all. `Scene.metadata` now carries the structural record (the plan, marker compliance, whether the scene was sectioned, where the seams fell, segments used, and whether it concluded naturally or was trimmed), which matters specifically because the section bounds say exactly where to look in the prose for a voice seam. Next came a calibration scene at 2,121 words against a 3,150 target: `expected_words()` had been sizing plans from per-mode means while the prompt advertised the p25 to p75 range, and on these right-skewed distributions the range midpoint sits 20 to 30% below the mean, so a writer obeying the prompt undershot by construction. Agreeing the two was not enough, because one range per mode is wrong regardless (masters' dialogue paragraphs run 6 words at the first decile to 94 at the ninth, and the calibration scene duly produced 43 metronomic ~20-word dialogue paragraphs), so each block now draws its own target from the measured decile ladder, deterministic per plan and rescaled to the mean-based total. The re-run went from 67% to 84% of target with paragraph spread 4 to 167. A live multi-tick run then lost tick 2, and its retry, to an `AttributeError` raised from inside `_parse_plan_response` when the tactical stage got `None` back from the backend; that now takes the same `_empty_plan()` path an unparseable response already took.

The last and most consequential read was of a four-scene run, where the step 7.6 tension rewrite turned out to be silently demolishing the paragraph plan. It fires after the writer has applied the skeleton and stripped its markers, and knows nothing about the plan, so it rewrote the prose whole: it hit two of four scenes and roughly halved their paragraph counts (64 to 36, 57 to 28) while eating about a fifth of the words. A scene written to a paragraph plan is no longer offered a free prose rewrite. Suite 889 to 900 across the four commits.

**Decisions & notes:** Two compliance metrics were found lying on the same day, and both were fixed to describe the artifact that actually reached disk. The marker check only verified that every plan item appeared, so a re-run was recorded compliant at 60/60 while running to 65 paragraphs with five items split; it now requires both halves, every plan item written and nothing written that was not one. Worse, `skeleton_compliance` described the pre-rewrite draft, reporting 57 paragraphs for a scene saved with 28, which is precisely the number a validation run would have cited as proof the plan was followed; it is now recounted at commit time against the saved text and flags `rewritten_after_planning` on a mismatch. Suppressing the rewrite is the narrow fix and is acknowledged as such: making the revision plan-aware is the better one and was deliberately left out, the justification being that the skeleton is the stronger structural claim and that arc-pressure's own finding is that events set the tension floor, not prose. That fix also revises the run's headline number: the pooled 65% of word target splits into 79% for the two scenes the rewrite never touched and 51% for the two it did, so the sizing work is in better shape than the pooled figure suggested. The remaining gap there is model compliance rather than arithmetic, with the writer still landing ~14% under each block's stated target and splitting long blocks.

## 2026-09-11

The previous day's Gate A re-run against the production sampler had left one failing check at the production default (DIALOGUE share 0.510 against the masters' 0.565), and this morning was spent diagnosing it and acting on the answer. The diagnosis is that it is a length effect, not a sampler defect: dialogue share climbs with plan size (0.365 at 400 words, 0.509 at 1400, 0.540 at 3150), because the grammar measures the chapter opener per chapter, and the opener is SETTING/ACTION/LORE with dialogue only reaching its base rate about four blocks in. Applying that opener to every scene is right, since scene maps to chapter 1:1; applying it to a 1400-word chapter is not. Measured over the same 659 aligned chapters, a masters chapter averages 3,493 words (median 3,165, p10 1,595), while the shipped targets are 400/800/1400/2200: the house default sits below the corpus p10 and the house maximum sits below the per-chapter average of all 21 books. So `generation.scene_length_preset` landed, mirroring `coherence.curve_preset` exactly (including its precedence rule, since the generated `config.yaml` writes the shipped targets into every project and so presence cannot mean customization), with a `masters` preset at 1,600/2,150/3,150/4,450 under which the sampler scores 24/24 at scene scale.

That immediately raised the question the preset cannot answer on its own, which is that a single request will not reliably return a 3,150-word chapter. Hence Slice 5, sectioned writing: the scene is now written across several plan-addressed calls, each owning a contiguous range of the skeleton's `[n]` blocks, so a 3,150-word plan becomes roughly five calls of 500 to 800 words, a target a model actually delivers. `partition_skeleton` splits the plan (a trailing stub merges into its predecessor, because a one-block final call would have to open, sustain and land at once), `section_word_target` sizes each call from its own mode mix, and every call sees the whole brief plus all prose so far but only its slice of the plan, with position instructions so exactly one section lands the ending. The plan rules are now shared with the single-shot prompt rather than duplicated. Suite 867 to 889 across the two commits.

**Decisions & notes:** Both flags are default off, and deliberately so for different reasons. The length preset is unchanged by default because adopting it roughly doubles prose per tick, which is a cost and pacing call rather than a technical one. Slice 5 is off because its risks (seam quality, voice continuity across section boundaries, cost per scene) are all unvalidated and need the measured A/B the landing sketch specifies, now carried as its own roadmap item; `_write_in_sections` returns None on any failure so the caller writes single-shot exactly as before, meaning the flag can cost quality but never a scene. Slice 5's original scoping was per-sub-block generation to test a richness hypothesis, which the Slice 4 production runs already answered "not needed for gpt-5.5"; the motivation shipped here is length instead, so the unit is a section, with `subblock_section_blocks: 1` recovering the literal per-block mode. The honest limit on the whole day's work: all of it measures the *plan*. Whether prose written from a masters-length plan resembles the corpus is untested, and the only judged evidence remains Slice 4's four scenes per arm at the house default.

## 2026-09-10

A single long day spent on the block-grammar line, opening with the overdue rewrite of `CLAUDE.md`, which had drifted two feature waves behind the code because the automated sweeps target README/ROADMAP/docs and leave the file that actually steers agent sessions alone. It was rebuilt against the source: the tick loop renumbered to the real `# Step N` markers with every missing step restored (construction-pressure detector, the sacred finale's ownership of the beat ask and its re-roll/end-marker/quarantine/expiry steps, judged extractor resolutions and judged loop closure, thread attribution), beat verification corrected from step 12 to 11.5, three new sections (threads, scene skeletons plus the write-until-concluded loop, export), the config knob groups checked against `DEFAULT_CONFIG`, and the inline shipped-list cut back to a pointer at ROADMAP so it cannot rot the same way again.

The craft fix came next: skeleton-guided scenes were packing five or six speech turns into one DIALOGUE paragraph, the top item from the Slice 4 reading notes and the suspected cause of the skeleton arm's low excursion-return rate. Measuring first showed the packing clause was a symptom. The nd1 sidecars carry labels but no word counts, so `block_grammar_tables.py` now aligns each book's labels back to the extracted markdown (all 21 align, accepting only an offset that reproduces every unit's paragraph count exactly) and emits `paragraph_words`. Paragraph length is a property of the mode: DIALOGUE 22 words at the median against LORE's 85. That made the old flat divisor arithmetically impossible to satisfy, since 16 dialogue paragraphs at masters' lengths are worth 663 words and a 1400-word target could only be reached by overfilling them. `generate_skeleton` now fills a word budget from measured per-mode lengths, each item renders with its own word range, and consecutive DIALOGUE items are one turn each. A reporting bug fell out alongside it: `judge_scenes.py` had hardcoded the masters' words-per-paragraph at 90, which was gate_c's shared budget convention rather than a corpus statistic, so the Slice 4 table had reported a 70% overshoot as a near-hit.

The afternoon closed gap 1 of the masters study by inducing the scene layer, and the answer was that Section 7's argument for a hidden layer compared against the wrong null. The excursion-return excess (0.355 observed against a first-order 0.238) is reproduced almost exactly by a plain second-order chain at 0.353, cross-validated by book across four folds. No fitted HMM reaches the observed rate or beats second-order at any state count, though three induced states do read as scene types and land close to constants already shipping. Incidentally, the shipped sampler's scene layer earns none of the return rate; its one decisive advantage is the chapter boundary. That prompted a same-day Gate A re-run against the production sampler rather than the PoC's own: 25/25 at chapter scale, 23/24 at the 1400-word production default, where the single failure is a length effect (the grammar's *chapter* opener distribution is applied to every scene, so orienting material gets paid for every ~23 blocks instead of once across ~58).

**Decisions & notes:** The L2 negative result is narrow and was explicitly scoped in docs after being questioned: it says a hidden scene state is invisible to the writer given two blocks of context, not that the DSL does not need scenes. What is retired is *inducing* L2 from block labels; block labels carry nothing of a scene in the authorial sense (same place, cast, purpose), so a plan can be scene-shaped by every statistic in the document while the prose wanders in intent. The commitment measurement (carrier set-piece length in words) does not favor the scene layer either, but the hybrid's skeletons averaged 42 blocks against the corpus's 58.4, so it is confounded and recorded as a reason to run the experiment, not as a result; the Gate A re-run must carry a commitment measure at matched lengths. Two methodological notes worth keeping: Baum-Welch collapses from about half of random starts on this data and a four-restart pass produced a spurious pattern that vanished at six, so the default is ten restarts with a test pinning it; and both Gate A scales are reported rather than one, since the chapter figure is the like-for-like gate against a per-chapter corpus while the scene figure is what production actually emits. The skeleton flag stays default-off, and the predicted lift in return rate is *not* validated: it needs a re-run, and the PoC harness's writer prompt still carries the old flat rules. Suite 844 to 867.

## 2026-07-12

A dense Phase 3 day that turned the latest validation runs into shipped fixes and opened the thread-interleaving build. The centerpiece was killing scene truncation: the grant-rate run found the writer's flat 3000-token ceiling was cutting 8 of 16 scenes off mid-sentence, a standing page-quality defect that had also cost a judged-closure grant and an end marker on run 5. The new invariant is that no scene commits without a detected ending (natural, concluded-on-request, or trimmed to the last complete sentence and flagged). It arrived in four layers: word targets coordinated with token ceilings so the model is finally told the length constraint it could never see; `finish_reason` plumbed through the api backend with per-provider normalization; a pure completion heuristic for backends that expose no metadata; and a sequential write-until-concluded loop that carries the whole scene so far with a firm conclude-and-stop instruction, capped at `scene_max_segments`. The segment plumbing was built deliberately so the block DSL's per-block writing mode (Slice 5) can reuse it.

On top of that came a six-fix hardening batch driven by evidence from the runs: `loop_resolved` was un-gated as an authorable contract check now that its grant rate (77.8%) cleared the bar, with finale slots exempted and a refused claim routing to keep-pending/rolling-horizon as real enforcement; `llm.timeout` was made to actually govern the api backend per request on every provider (it had been inert, letting a hung server hang up to 22 minutes, now failing in ~4.6s); beat-level dedup landed to kill the 9200-verbatim-character finding, using `max(plain, sorted-token)` difflib ratio because plain ratio could not separate duplicates from legitimate neighbors; the loop-dedup threshold was tuned to 0.75 against two documented near-misses; finale-refusal reasons are now persisted and printed; and the END marker is guaranteed on sacred-finale scenes with an import-time assert locking it to the completion heuristic's recognizer.

The last feature commit added a construction-pressure detector (Slice T4a, instrument-only: it computes when thread construction *would* fire and records it in metrics, with no effect on planning yet) and curve presets (`coherence.curve_preset`, default "house" byte-identical to the shipped curve, plus opt-in thriller-register, wind-down, and domestic-arc presets traced to the masters study's decile tables), routing every curve reader including the sacred finale through one resolver. The accompanying spec revision, informed by the masters corpus, reframed the B-thread as constructed for structural/POV variety and convergence payoff rather than as a calm reservoir, demoted the demand-gap trigger to an opt-in experiment, and derived `thread_min_run` from the masters' block proportions.

**Decisions & notes:** The relief cut is now treated as an authored, budgeted device (masters used it only ~3 times across 149 chapters), not a routine tension valve, which is why construction pressure ships as instrument-only first. Curve presets deliberately route the finale's target and cap through the same resolver so a thriller preset's hot climax is not re-rolled toward calm. The un-gating of `loop_resolved` is the first real loop-closure enforcement to ship, the pressure the log has been pointing at for weeks.

## 2026-07-10

A large single-day burst, driven end to end by a sequence of plot-first "descent" validation runs (aiming the arc at a calm resolution and watching where it breaks). Three runs' worth of findings turned into concrete fixes. From the contracts smoke run: a stochastic malformed-JSON response was poisoning the beat horizon by dropping into a line fallback that turned raw JSON source lines into persistent beat descriptions (now retries once and rejects code-fragment "beats"), and LLM contract authoring had never actually fired because the prompt's authoritative shape block omitted `postconditions`, so a schema-obedient model always omitted them. From the descent run proper, three more defects: the beat-generation token budget was hardcoded at 1000 and truncated every contracts-plus-arc-guidance batch (new `generation.beat_max_tokens`, default 2000), two checkers that can never pass today (`char_at_location`, `loop_resolved`) were steering the prompt toward wedging beats on impossible conditions (gated out of authoring, still registered for evaluation), and contract verification was moved to tick step 11.5 so state-reading checks see the post-scene world. Two further backstops followed from the clean re-run: `TENSION_FLOOR_CAP = 7` clamps any `tension_at_least` floor above the scorer's practical ceiling (an unsatisfiable floor had pinned the whole resolution window), and a beat that has gone stale off the curve no longer suppresses the schedule's guidance, so a stale peak can't hijack the finale. Finally, slot alignment: batches now cap to the remaining story runway (`min(count, L - T)`) so the last authored beat lands on the final tick and inherits the curve endpoint, with an explicit no-new-threats final-scene clause — fixing the arbiter run's denouement-scheduled-past-the-end bug.

Alongside the fixes, two larger threads. First, an empirical **block decomposition study**: paragraph-level structural annotation of six public-domain masters (Buchan, Eddison, Conrad, Austen, Haggard, Dunsany, ~970 paragraphs) against the generated novels, cross-validated across models (kappa 0.75, 8/8 blind probes). The headline conclusions matured over the day: block *proportions* are not the gap (generated prose sits inside the six-master distribution spread, and bulk distribution is confirmed useless as a gauge), the gap is *rhythm and commitment* — masters sustain long carrier set-pieces (565–1618 word runs vs 204–346) and exit interiority instead of looping it (master self-transition 14% vs generated 40%). The load-bearing DSL constraint that falls out is the interiority-exit rule. Second, a new **thread-interleaving design**: since the fork experiment proved the tension overshoot lives in the *assigned event*, not the writer prompt (pruning 80% of the writer prompt moved mean tension by 0.0), the tension curve is reframed as a scene-selection policy over a portfolio of threads — calm pages come from cutting away with a cliffhanger, not from becalming a hot event. The falsified prompt-surgery roadmap item was retired in favor of this.

Also shipped: **OpenRouter support** in the `api` backend (own OpenAI-compatible client singleton, `OPENROUTER_API_KEY`/`OPENROUTER_MODEL`, first test coverage for `multi_provider_llm`, live-validated on an 8-tick run). ROADMAP/CLAUDE.md/EMERGENT_COHERENCE_PLAN synced with the whole burst.

**Decisions & notes:** The two never-satisfiable checkers stay *registered in the evaluation engine* but are gated out of *authoring* — the vocabulary the prompt can draw from is deliberately narrower than what the engine can score, so beats can't be authored against conditions nothing in the pipeline can yet make true. The "4.5x-closer" proportions claim from the first pass was explicitly retired once the corpus grew to six masters — a good example of an early headline dissolving under more data. The wedged-beat problem is left deliberately un-auto-fixed (give-up/skip rules risk drifting off-outline); the elimination chain across the descent runs now points squarely at thread interleaving plus resolution sequencing as the real fix, not more prompt or rewrite pressure.

## 2026-07-09

Attacked the one failure the June validation runs kept showing: arc-pressure tracks rising tension targets but cannot de-escalate for a resolution, because the planner keeps choosing tense events no matter what number it is aimed at. The fix is the arc-phase planner mandate (Phase 3): the target tension curve is now also read as a story phase (rising, peak, falling, resolution), and the planner gets a firm event-level mandate per phase (escalate, confront, resolve) instead of only a numeric target. The mandate covers both the Stage 1 strategic path and plot-first's `plan_for_beat` (injected into the Stage 3 tactical prompt, suppressed when the beat carries its own `tension_target`). The bounded tension rewrite is now skipped when the target drop is large enough that different events, not different prose, are needed (`rewrite_futile`), and `arc_phase` is recorded in the per-tick coherence rubric. Alongside this, a new design doc (`docs/BLOCKS_CONTRACTS_LANDING_SKETCH.md`) reconciles the 2025 blocks/contracts architecture proposal with what has actually shipped, cutting the remaining work into slices: contracts riding on plot beats as the MVP, then precondition pressure, bounded contract repair, scene skeletons, and a measured per-sub-block generation experiment.

The sketch's first slices did not stay on paper long. The same day, beat generation itself was taught the curve: each beat in a generated batch is scheduled at its future tick, given an interpolated tension target and a phase directive in the generation prompt, and authored targets are reconciled to the schedule after parsing (clamp to a plus or minus 2 band, fill when missing). On top of that, contracts Slice 1 landed: `PlotBeat` now carries pre/postconditions authored at beat-generation time from the closed checker vocabulary, sanitized in both generation paths (unknown checks and phantom refs dropped, tension conditions reconciled against the beat's own target), evaluated during beat verification (a pass upgrades confidence, a failure routes to the existing keep-pending and horizon-revision paths), and measured in the coherence rubric. The stale `contracts.json` store and `ContractManager` are retired. A validation re-run of the June descent scenario confirmed the mandate works where it matters: the final scene dropped from 8/10 to 6/10 against a target of 4, with the planner demonstrably choosing aftermath events instead of confrontations (`docs/progress_report_20260709.md`).

**Decisions & notes:** The mandate is gated by `coherence.arc_phase_mandate` (default True; False restores exact pre-mandate behavior for A/B runs), but the rubric records `arc_phase` ungated so on/off runs stay comparable. The mandate's known gap at commit time (plot beat generation not receiving the phase) was closed the same day by the beat-generation bridge. Contracts stay behind `generation.use_contracts` (default False) until a plot-first smoke run exercises them live. On contracts, the sanitizer enforces consistency with the beat's tension rather than prohibiting contracts from authoring tension conditions. Two validation findings worth carrying forward: the rising phase ran hotter than the June control (possibly the escalate mandate amplifying the genre's hot bias), and the resolution mandate's close-open-loops clause did not bite (8 loops opened, 0 closed), which is direct evidence for the planned loop-aging pressure.
