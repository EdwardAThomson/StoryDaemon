# Roadmap — StoryDaemon

_Status: active · updated 2026-09-01_

An agentic long-form fiction generator: an autonomous LLM agent runs iterative
"story tick" cycles (plan → tools → write → evaluate) that grow characters, lore,
and plot emergently rather than from a pre-plan. Python, multi-backend. The active
strategic plan is `docs/EMERGENT_COHERENCE_PLAN.md`.

- [x] **Scene layer (L2) induction, negative result**: the excursion-return excess that Section 7 of the masters study read as evidence for a level above the block is second-order memory, not hierarchy. A plain second-order chain reproduces it (0.353 vs the masters' 0.355, cross-validated across four book-level folds) where first-order gives 0.234, and no fitted HMM matches it at any state count tried. Three recognisable scene types do emerge and sit close to the constants already shipping. Tooling: `scripts/scene_layer_hmm.py` (LLM-free). Closes gap 1 of the study's next steps: it retires inducing L2 *from block labels*, not the idea of a scene. Scenes are real and scene-sized here (the induced conversation state runs 1,000 to 1,500 words), and the scene question that matters is about *intent* (same place, cast, purpose), which block labels cannot see at all and which needs a different annotation pass to measure. Note the scope: all of this concerns the plan *generator*, not the proportion control the DSL exists for, which lives in the writer prompt and is unaffected. Details in `docs/MASTERS_BLOCK_GRAMMAR_STUDY.md` section 12
- [x] **Chapter-length calibration** (`generation.scene_length_preset`, default `house` and byte-identical): Gate A run against the *production* sampler for the first time (`experiments/block_grammar_poc/gate_a_production.py`) passes 25/25 at chapter scale but fails DIALOGUE share at the 1400-word default (0.510 vs 0.565). It is a length effect: scene maps to chapter 1:1, the grammar's opening orientation is measured per chapter, and the house targets sit below the corpus p10 with the house *maximum* below the per-chapter average of every book in it (masters median 3,165 words; shortest book 1,989). The opt-in `masters` preset (1,600/2,150/3,150/4,450) scores 24/24. Default unchanged: adopting it roughly doubles prose per tick, a cost and pacing decision. Details in `docs/MASTERS_BLOCK_GRAMMAR_STUDY.md` section 13
- [x] **Masters block grammar + evaluation harness**: block-transition grammar, chapter boundary rules, run lengths, and the excursion-return kernel measured on the 21-masterwork nd1 corpus (`docs/MASTERS_BLOCK_GRAMMAR_STUDY.md`, data in `novel_agent/data/block_grammar_v1.json`, 26-book refresh pre-validated); reusable gates methodology in `experiments/block_grammar_poc/` (statistical Gate A, compliance Gate B, outcome-A/B Gate C, corpus-judge scene scoring)
- [x] **Scene skeletons (contracts Slice 4)**, `generation.enable_scene_skeleton` (default off): typed paragraph plan sampled from the masters grammar (planner-authored `scene_skeleton` wins when present), sized by the scene word target, tension-conditioned, carried in the writer prompt with per-item [n] markers, stripped with compliance recording (both halves: every plan item written, and nothing written that was not a plan item; recounted at commit time against the text actually saved, flagging `rewritten_after_planning` on a mismatch). The plan, its compliance, the section seams and the segment accounting are persisted on the committed scene's `metadata`, so a finished scene can be audited block by block. A scene written to a paragraph plan is not offered the step 7.6 whole-scene prose rewrite, which knew nothing about the plan and collapsed it; it gets the selective paragraph revision below instead. Production A/B vs unguided (gpt-5.5): every solidly measured block statistic moved toward the masters; 16/16 marker compliance on all production scenes; no surface regression (`docs/SLICE4_SCENE_SKELETON_RESULTS.md`)

## Next

- [ ] **Loop-aging pressure** (older open loops surface louder for payoff) — *the gauge landed, the pressure has not.* Slice L1 ships `agent/loop_aging.py`, a `loop_age` payload per tick, `novel loops`, and the corpus baseline in `docs/LOOP_AGING_BASELINE.md`; nothing reads any of it for decisions. What the baseline changed about the job: across 26 novels only 220 of 1,548 loops ever closed (14%), and closure is near-immediate or never (60% of closures land within three ticks of birth), but **a third of the ledger is not debt at all** — scene-local suspense that carries its own deadline ("before she leaves the building"), which the story answers by continuing and the closure judge rightly refuses to close, plus 4% that restate the story's own throughline and are meant to stay open to the last page, plus POV-asymmetric questions the reader already knows the answer to (one surveillance-POV scene minted six). So every loop now carries a `horizon` and only `arc` loops can be overdue. Two candidate pressures, and the recorded series is meant to decide between them rather than taste: (a) age-weighted selection plus an overdue section, scaled by `compute_arc_phase` so it does not fight the arc-phase mandate; (b) creation-side hygiene that stops minting the noise, which is cheaper and may buy more. The number to read first is `loop_shown_oldest_age` against `loop_unshown_oldest_age`, because `_filter_relevant_loops` ranks by overlap with the intention the planner just wrote and therefore hides the oldest loops by construction. Classifier honesty: 70% agreement with a 60-loop hand-labelled sample, and its errors over-claim the two exempt horizons, so `loop_overdue_total` is a floor on the real debt
- [ ] **Thread interleaving (tension by scene selection)** — *partly landed:* the registry, thread identity by selection, and the construction-pressure detector are in (see Shipped); construction itself (Slice T4b) and selection are not. The tension curve becomes a scene-selection policy over a portfolio of story threads; a calm page comes from cutting away to a calmer thread (leaving a cliffhanger), never from becalming a hot event. Design: `docs/THREAD_INTERLEAVING_DESIGN.md`. Replaces the "forced low-tension scenes" idea, which the fork experiment falsified (`progress_report_20260710.md` Addendum 2: pruning 80 percent of the writer prompt moved mean tension by 0.0; the overshoot lives in the assigned event, not the prompt). Empirical grounding now available: the masters corpus thread-architecture data (thread counts, run lengths, convergence shapes) and the nd1 26-book reference bands
- [ ] Contracts Slice 2: precondition pressure (unmet preconditions become planner setup pressure at beat selection, never a hard raise)
- [ ] Contracts Slice 3: bounded contract repair (mirror the tension-rewrite pattern) plus an `event_occurs` LLM-judge checker
- [ ] Gate A re-run against a sampler with `_SCENE_TYPES` removed (second-order kernel plus measured openers/closers only). Section 12 finds the scene layer earns none of the return rate and costs a little accuracy on shares and run lengths, but Gate A's 25/25 was scored against the hybrid, so the comparison has to be made on the same instrument first. The run must add a *commitment* measure (set-piece length) at matched sequence lengths: commitment is the gap the decomposition study named, Gate A's current checks would not notice a sampler that stopped sustaining set-pieces, and the one measurement taken so far is confounded by skeleton length
- [x] **Sectioned scene writing (contracts Slice 5)**, `generation.subblock_generation` (default off): the scene is written across several plan-addressed calls, each owning a contiguous range of the skeleton's `[n]` blocks and seeing all prose so far, sized from its own mode mix. Motivated by the chapter-length finding rather than the original richness hypothesis: a single request does not reliably produce a masters-length chapter, and a 3,150-word plan becomes five calls of 500-800 words each. `subblock_section_blocks` sets granularity (1 = literal per-sub-block). Requires `enable_scene_skeleton`; falls back to single-shot on any failure. **Unvalidated:** seam quality, voice continuity and cost per scene all need the measured A/B the landing sketch specifies
- [x] **Selective paragraph revision** (DSL Slice 5, the repair-pass half): when a scene follows a paragraph plan, the tension rewrite revises only the tension-carrying paragraphs (chosen from the corpus's measured tension-band shares) and leaves the rest byte-identical, so structure, length and marker compliance survive by construction. Replaces the whole-scene rewrite that was silently undoing Slice 4. **Unvalidated:** a plan-constrained revision may move tension less than a free one, which the keep-only-if-closer rule makes safe but not necessarily useful
- [ ] Measured A/B for sectioned writing per `docs/BLOCKS_CONTRACTS_LANDING_SKETCH.md` Slice 5: prose-richness judge, voice-continuity judge across section seams, cost per scene, and a human read, against skeleton-guided single-shot on the same foundation. Promote only if richness wins and voice continuity holds. Note what the mode is *not* for: the original richness hypothesis was answered "not needed for gpt-5.5" by the Slice 4 production runs (single-shot honors paragraph fullness), so the case to prove here is length
- [ ] Skeleton refinements from the Slice 4 reading notes. **Dialogue-run paragraphing is built, not yet validated:** paragraph length is now measured per mode on the masters corpus (`paragraph_words` in the grammar, study section 5b), every plan item draws its own word target from that mode's measured decile ladder (deterministic per plan, rescaled so the plan's total stays unbiased; one fixed range per mode either brackets the median and under-sizes every scene or brackets the mean and forbids the short turns that are half the corpus), consecutive DIALOGUE items are one speech turn each, and the sampler fills a word budget instead of dividing by a flat 90. The predicted lift in excursion-return rate needs a re-run to confirm, and the PoC harness's writer prompt has to be synced to the production one first. Still open: genre-conditioned grammars; MTLD over-diversity as a candidate metric

## Backlog

- [ ] Phase 4 — setup/payoff foresight (planted-element ledger for Chekhov's guns)
- [ ] Plan explicit low-tension beats if arc-pressure proves insufficient (note: Slice 4 skeleton sampling already tension-conditions the prose-level block mix; this item concerns beat selection, which it does not touch)
- [ ] Name-bank registers are surname-only (noted, not critical): `_lasts()` honours a `register` argument, `_firsts()` ignores it, so given name and surname are drawn independently. Harmless for the Victorian bank, whose `gentry`/`common` split is one population stratified by class sharing a given-name pool. **Westerns are where it bites**, because a western cast is not one population but several — Anglo settlers, Californio and Mexican-American families, Irish immigrants (Callahan, Donnelly, Driscoll, Rafferty, Kearney and Halloran are already in the bank), Chinese railroad labour, German farmers, Black cowboys — and which one a character belongs to governs the given name as much as the surname. Adding a Spanish register today would yield "Wyatt Vega" as readily as "Ramón Vega". Related data gap: the western bank holds two Spanish-family names and no Spanish surnames, while 39 sit unused in the scifi and modern banks. Fix when wanted is small — give `_firsts` the same register lookup `_lasts` has (~5 lines) and author per-register given names; the tool already exposes `register`, so the LLM selects it per character, which is the Phase 1 pattern. A weighted population mix was considered and set aside: the weights would be a historical claim with no gauge behind them, against this project's ship-the-gauge-first habit. Mixed families are real, so any register constraint should be a strong default rather than a rule
- [ ] **Loop mention tracking is dead, and it silently disables emergent story goals** (found during loop-aging Slice L1, deliberately not fixed there): `OpenLoop.scenes_mentioned` and `last_mentioned_tick` are read in three places and written in none, so both are 0/None on all 1,548 loops on disk. Two consequences. `_check_goal_promotion` (ticks 10 to 15) requires a loop mentioned 5 or more times, so **it can never fire**: no run has ever auto-promoted a story goal, and `novel goals` always prints "Mentioned in 0 scenes". And the loop-aging classifier's throughline test degrades to an idiom guess, because its sound branch is similarity to `state.story_goals.primary`, which is almost never set. The fix needs a decision about the signal's source: beat `advances_loops` claims (already sanitized and stored, but plot-first only, which is off by default) or a new `loops_addressed` field on the tactical plan (works in both modes, but `llm.planner_max_tokens` is load-bearing and must rise with it). L1 records loop *exposure* to the planner instead, which is free and exact
- [ ] Wedged-beat problem (noted, deliberately not auto-fixed): a beat whose contract keeps failing retries forever when `allow_beat_skip` and `rolling_horizon` are both off (observed live: 4 consecutive failures on one beat, 2026-07-10 smoke run). A bounded give-up rule was considered and rejected as dangerous (silently drifting off-outline); the preferred fix is making scenes comply with their tension targets (see forced low-tension scenes) so contracts stop failing in the first place
